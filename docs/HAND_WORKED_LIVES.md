# Hand-worked lives — small material stories before industrial production

This direct-craft increment uses the current gap before large industrial asset workflows
to author details that should remain intentional even when later production becomes highly automated.

## Adult Ranjit reference

The user-supplied adult Ranjit Singh image is recorded by content digest in
`data/art/ranjit_reference_direction.json`. The image itself is not redistributed.

The reference is used as **adult visual direction**, not as authentication of every
garment, color, jewel or date. Its strongest motifs are a comparatively restrained
light layered silhouette, compact wrapped headwear, dark facial hair, visible sword,
limited ornament and composed standing presence.

Childhood Buddh does **not** inherit adult facial hair, court costume or adult proportions.
The intended life-course direction is continuity through posture, headwear logic,
weapon habit and eventually face—not dressing the child as a miniature Maharaja.

## Three optional material stories

### The Pale Repair

A basket has three pale replacement strips that intentionally do not match the older
dark wicker. Hand-contact areas are lighter. Small unused offcuts were retained instead
of discarded.

### Colour Under the Fold

A folded cloth is brighter where light did not reach it. Pale repair thread crosses
different dyes without disguising the repair. Two differently filled thread spools
suggest continued use rather than a pristine merchant display.

### A Place Kept

A nicked empty cup sits beside a rolled cloth. Older cup rings remain on the board.
The scene suggests absence or expectation without assigning an identity, death, secret,
quest, or historical claim.

All dialogue around these objects is original fiction. None of the details creates
loot, money, a codex unlock, reputation, morality, relationship points or a saved
historical fact.

## Why these details remain direct craft

A future production pipeline can manufacture basket variants, cloth piles and cups.
It should not decide **which** repair is conspicuous, why an unused offcut was kept,
whether an empty place should be explained, or how long a player should be allowed
to wonder.

The rule is:

```text
industrial workflow → families / variants / validation
human direction      → meaningful exception / placement / implication
```

The same principle should later govern sword-scabbard wear, repaired tack, old plaster
near habitual hand contact, asymmetrical court furniture, inherited jewelry, patched
campaign clothing and objects that remain after their owner is absent.

## Historical boundary

These three bazaar details are fictional micro-worldbuilding. They are not claimed as
documented objects from Ranjit Singh's childhood or surveyed Gujranwala material culture.
Period-nearer evidence must still govern final architecture, costume, weapons and trade
material.

The adult Ranjit reference likewise supplies art-direction motifs only. It does not make
later photographic/studio conventions valid for 1792.

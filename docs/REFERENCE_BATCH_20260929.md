# Landscape, military and Ranjit appearance reference intake

Status: design direction and research leads, not historical admission or new assets.

The supplied environment views establish foliage/water transitions, layered urban
facades, ground detail, foreground shadow and distant silhouette quality targets.
They do not establish vegetation or architecture for a particular Punjabi place
and date. Do not turn Gujranwala into the pictured oasis or fortified fantasy city.

The miniature photographs are secondary modelling interpretations. Their useful
leads are differing mounted silhouettes, equipment, formation spacing, artillery
and fieldworks. Their captions, flags, clothing and force composition are not
primary evidence. In particular, later regular-army references do not enter the
1792 childhood scene simply because they depict Sikh soldiers. The character art
and sculpt views are material/silhouette references, not authenticated portraits.
They are not automatically assigned to Ranjit or to a named religious figure.

## Ranjit's own presentation

The user's default character direction is an uncovered face, plain everyday
clothing, a cloth turban and self-possessed bearing. Do not hide the left-eye
condition or pockmarks behind an invented assassin hood, mask or blindfold.
Do not automatically copy the oversized martial turban, ornate armour, adult face
or beard of a reference into the childhood model. The current fitted costume is
an unmasked plain-cloth study; its final facial features and scarring are pending.

The supporting evidence needs careful wording. SikhMuseum.com's headgear study
reproduces descriptions by Vigne, Fane and Hugel. It supports comparatively simple
attire and turban use, but also records jewellery and a travelling episode with
cloth wrapped around the chin. Thus "never any facial covering" and "never any
jewellery or armour" are not established historical absolutes. Ordinary covered
travel/weather arrangements are different from concealing his identity or scars.

Source: https://www.sikhmuseum.com/helmet/ranjit/ranjitsingh.html
The page gives primary-account bibliographic leads; those books should be checked
before authenticating quotations or a particular 1792 garment. Later observations
must not silently become eyewitness descriptions of his childhood.

The Wallace Collection's OA1769 turban helmet is dated 1825-50 and discussed with
an 1831 troop review. It informs later specialist military equipment, not Ranjit's
default childhood headgear. Owning or depicting a type does not prove everyone
wore it or that he wore it in a specific scene.
Source: https://www.wallacecollection.org/explore/explore-in-depth/the-sikh-empire/sikh-arms-armour/spotlight-arms-armour/turban-helmet/

The one-eye/equal-treatment anecdote is retained as an attributed story lead.
The supplied Sikhchic page is a 2011 poem introduction; this intake has not traced
its wording to a contemporary account. It may be used through the established
oral-account system with that status, not silently authenticated verbatim speech.
Source: https://www.sikhchic.com/article-detail.php?id=2096&cat=12

# Inhabited bazaar approach — texture without simulating everything

This pass develops the physical walk between the existing market invitation and confrontation.

The goal is not another systemic NPC layer. It is to make the route feel occupied before the dramatic event begins.

## Three ambient vignettes

The existing path now passes three original prototype studies:

1. **goods handling** — two generic figures work around baskets and folded cloth;
2. **tethered pack-animal study** — a generic animal shifts its head and tail beside a post;
3. **stationary loading cart** — sacks and wheels create another layer at the edge of the route.

None of these are documented historical people, surveyed market arrangements, merchants with inventories, simulated witnesses, trade-price sources, interactable shops or navigation bodies.

They contain no collision or AI authority. Their motion is presentation sampled from the existing game clock.

## Companion observations

During the existing `invited` phase, Mela and Jiva can comment on the space while physically following Buddh.

The lines are deliberately ordinary:

> Jiva: Mind the baskets. If you knock one over, I am leaving you to explain it.

> Mela: Look at that one. I would take him over your pony.

> Jiva: You say that about every animal you have not fallen off yet.

These are original fictional lines. They do not create journal memories or relationship variables.

A vignette is session-local and only triggers when:
- the player physically reaches its small zone,
- the outing is still in the pre-confrontation walk,
- both friends remain close enough for the existing contact rule,
- no more important encounter dialogue is currently speaking.

If a save rewinds to before a line, hearing it again is acceptable because the whole world was explicitly rewound. No new persistence schema is introduced.

## Environmental performance

Before confrontation, the goods handlers have small working gestures. Once fighting starts, their motions become stiller rather than launching a crowd simulation or scripted panic state.

The animal and cart remain ambient visual studies. They do not become physics bodies during combat.

This preserves a useful production rule:

```text
distant/ambient occupation = presentation
interacted person/object    = candidate for authoritative state
```

We should promote background entities into simulation only when gameplay actually requires identity, custody, memory, collision or consequences.

## What a terminal can and cannot establish

Automation can check:
- no collision was introduced,
- no state changed,
- zones are bounded,
- dialogue only appears in the intended phase,
- repeated sampling remains harmless.

It cannot determine:
- whether the route feels busy enough,
- whether the lines are charming or irritating,
- whether the animal silhouette looks convincing,
- whether the composition draws the player toward the confrontation,
- whether the background is too visually noisy.

Those require play, observation and revision.

## Next craft threshold

After this approach pass, the remaining gap is increasingly **quality of authored assets and performances**, not missing control-plane machinery: skinned characters, facial acting, recorded voices, sourced market architecture/materials, real animal/cart assets, surface-specific footsteps and a final environmental mix.

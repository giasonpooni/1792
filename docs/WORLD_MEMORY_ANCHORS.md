# World memory anchors — authored exceptions industrial generation must preserve

The visual corpus is now large enough to support broad families of streets, courts,
weapons, textiles and furniture. That makes one risk more important: **generated
consistency can erase evidence of individual use**.

This increment therefore hand-authors six architectural/material exceptions in the
current bazaar street study.

They are not quests, collectibles or historical claims. Their value is that somebody
made a specific repair or repeatedly used one specific place.

## Six anchors

### Threshold hand polish

Only one side of one raised threshold is smoother/lighter. Later generators may change
the surrounding threshold family but should not symmetrize this wear away.

### Replacement screen slat

One upper screen member uses visibly different wood and section. The repair is not
camouflaged.

### Replaced drain stone

A short drainage segment has a different material age. The street does not look as if
every surface was constructed yesterday from one texture set.

### Tether groove

Two narrow rope-contact bands mark one edge post. This is localized use, not a global
"dirt slider."

### Measuring notches

Six slightly unequal marks occur at one working edge. They imply repeated manual use
without creating a named merchant, quest clue or documented historic measurement system.

### Later plaster patch

One upper bay has a repair with a slightly different finish. A future facade generator
must treat the patch as an authored layer, not noise to homogenize.

## The preservation rule

```text
generator may replace the family
             │
             └── must preserve the meaningful exception
```

The exact meshes can later be rebuilt at higher fidelity. The **semantic exception**
survives until a human art review explicitly replaces it.

## Not historical evidence

The corpus supports durable visual ideas such as repaired screens, threshold wear,
drainage margins and layered plaster. These exact six placements are fictional
micro-worldbuilding.

They do not establish that a particular 1792 Gujranwala threshold, drain, screen,
tether point or measuring edge looked this way.

## Why the terminal should not choose them

A control plane is excellent at detecting that six anchors still exist after an asset
replacement. It is poor at deciding:

- that the wear should occur on this side rather than both;
- that one repair should deliberately mismatch;
- that only one post deserves a tether groove;
- that the marks should stop after six;
- that the patch should remain unexplained.

Those decisions are the authored irregularities that prevent a generated world from
feeling statistically plausible but personally empty.

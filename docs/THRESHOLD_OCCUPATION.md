# Threshold occupation — habitual use without another NPC system

The reference corpus repeatedly shows that streets and courts become legible through **where people actually spend time**: shade, raised thresholds, counters, water places and waiting edges.

This increment hand-authors four site-specific habits into the existing bazaar street section. They are presentation, not simulated professions.

## Four threshold habits

### Shade mender

A generic seated figure uses one shaded threshold with a patched mat, cloth and thread. The hand motion is small and stops when the encounter becomes a fight. There is no name, inventory, customer queue or production recipe.

### Cooling water place

A shaded vessel, damp ring, inverted cup and cloth cover imply repeated use even though nobody is currently standing there.

### Counting edge

A low board, unequal weights and one wrapped weight suggest manual counting at one edge. A generic seated figure makes a small repeated hand motion. No transactions are created.

### Empty waiting mat

A repaired mat and folded wrap remain prepared but empty. The game deliberately does not explain who normally waits there or whether anybody is expected.

## Why this belongs outside NET

A control plane can verify that these habits remain outside the central lane, that their IDs survive asset replacement, and that they mutate no world state.

It should not decide which threshold becomes a repair place, that the water user is absent, why one weight is wrapped, or that one waiting place remains unexplained.

Those are compositional and story choices.

## Later industrialization

A future crowd or street-occupation system may generate surrounding activity. These four habits remain authored exceptions. They are not templates to clone across every bazaar.

No part of this increment claims that these exact activities occupied these exact places in historical Gujranwala.

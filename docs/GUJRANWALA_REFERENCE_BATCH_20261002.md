## British Raj paper: retain the later historical layer

**Muhammad Kashif Ali, Javed-ur-Rehman and Ghulam Shabbir**, *A Socio-Cultural
Historical Survey of Gujranwala during the British Raj (1849–1947)*,
**Ancient Punjab 9, 2021, pp. 63–74**. Use the printed first page for cataloguing:
the embedded PDF title refers to an unrelated Gandhara architecture article and
its metadata author differs from the printed authors. Its 2022 file creation date
is separate from the 2021 volume year.

- **pp. 63–64:** a cattle/grazing origin tradition and changing nineteenth-century
  district boundaries are research leads, not a dated 1792 land-use inventory.
- **pp. 69–70:** Rabi/Kharif cultivation, chahi well-irrigated land, oxen, Persian
  wheels and carts are described in a colonial-period discussion. They support
  investigation of working agricultural landscapes, without independently dating
  an opening-period crop calendar, well location, irrigation coverage or yield.
- **p. 64 and pp. 70–71:** district and town populations, workshops, railways,
  regional crafts and timber movement carry different dates and geographic units.
  Keep those units attached; do not transfer later counts or religious composition
  into 1792.
- **pp. 73–74:** follow the cited gazetteers, Latifi, Douie and interviews back to
  their original editions before using precise quantities or chronology. Those
  underlying works were not newly verified in this intake.

This paper's colonial irrigation generalization does not undo the ecology rule
that cultivation, grazing livelihoods and irrigation existed before colonial
canal expansion. Nor do its historical social categories become fixed behavioural
rules for game characters.

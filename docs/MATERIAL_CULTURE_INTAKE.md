## Design proposals, not historical episodes

Immediate authored-fiction candidate: a household retainer accompanies young
Ranjit to collect repaired riding equipment. Conflicting memories of payment
create an obligation dispute. The scene links a maker, object, household, horse
and remembered agreement. It reuses interaction and social-memory systems rather
than adding an independent crafting economy. This is not a claimed biographical
episode and has not been implemented by this intake.

Later candidates: travelling-court reception, an envoy's gift, a storekeeper's
custody record, working relationships between local staff and new specialists,
and visits to officers' households. Register them within the full-life base-game
plan; do not launch DLC production or spawn later people into childhood.

Ordinary repaired vessels, work clothing, everyday tack and shaded courtyards
should receive visual care as well as exceptional court objects. Proposed
long-lived workshops/family continuity must be marked as original connective
fiction unless separately evidenced. Shared surnames do not establish descent.

## Asset and scene admission contract

Separate manufacture date, earliest attestation, regional presence, acquisition,
ownership and date of depiction. A later painting is not automatically evidence
of a childhood-era object's exact appearance; late attestation is not by itself
proof of late invention.

For each asset retain claim/source identity, applicable place/time, evidence
status, reconstruction decisions, reference-use rights, model version and intended
scene. Review silhouette, dimensions where known, materials, construction, wear,
rig and movement. Inspect multiple lighting conditions and actual gameplay views.
A geometric/material quality pass does not establish historical truth.

Religious sites remain exterior-only and religious figures unembodied. The
bounded prayer/fortune implementation is not changed by relic research. Sada Kaur
and Adina Beg remain non-playable. Present clothes through period, region,
occupation, means and context, not a universal religion/caste costume category.

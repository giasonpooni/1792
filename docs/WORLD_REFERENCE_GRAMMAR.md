# World reference grammar — what the visual corpus may and may not author

The growing user-supplied corpus is now large enough to become **design infrastructure**.
It is not a pile of images to be copied.

The corpus lives in `data/art/world_reference_corpus_20260930.json`. Image binaries
remain outside the repository; entries are bound by filename and SHA256 only.

## Four reference tiers

- **A — direct / source-authenticated.** Near-period object, building, drawing or record
  with enough provenance to constrain reconstruction.
- **B — continuity.** Later photograph or surviving fabric. Useful for durable massing,
  spatial relationships, wear and construction language, but later alterations cannot
  silently travel backward in time.
- **C — visual language.** Painting, genre image, secondary plate or insufficiently
  provenanced object. Useful for composition, silhouette and occupation.
- **D — motif only.** Modern model, costume styling, concept/studio image. Useful for
  presentation mechanics; historical truth contribution is zero.

The tier travels with any future generated derivative. A C/D input can never satisfy a
historical-authentication gate merely because an agent produced a convincing mesh.

## Street section

The strongest repeated urban rule is **dense edges / dynamic center**.

```text
upper screen / balcony / projecting room
               │
        shaded upper façade
               │
awnings / thresholds / raised plinths
               │
goods / seated work / tethered animal / repair
════════════ EDGE OCCUPATION ════════════
          drainage / worn margin

              OPEN LANE
     foot / horse / cart / procession

          drainage / worn margin
════════════ EDGE OCCUPATION ════════════
               │
      threshold / shop / shade
               │
upper screen / balcony / projection
```

Do not make density by permanently packing the center with NPCs. The center changes
temporally: open, horse, cart, quarrel, procession, cleared authority route.

## Threshold grammar

Repeated references show that a doorway is often an **environmental event**:

```text
dark / compressed space
        ↓
deep arch / screen
        ↓
controlled glimpse
        ↓
bright court / water / garden / authority
```

Use this for palaces, forts and exterior approaches to religious sites. Thresholds can
be memorable without making every interior enterable.

## Courtyard grammar

Court and garden references repeatedly use:

- a strong long axis;
- arcades defining perimeter shade;
- water as orientation, cooling and ceremony;
- rooms opening onto the court rather than isolated corridors;
- upper screening/projections;
- asymmetry from use even when the plan is formal.

Later restoration planting must not be mistaken for original planting.

## Fort and gate grammar

Fortification should be experienced as sequence rather than a single door:

```text
distant visibility → approach → wall mass → queue/control → compressed threshold
→ release into court → next authority layer
```

Who waits and who is waved through is political staging. Child Buddh, a merchant,
a courier, a senior sardār and later Ranjit should experience the same gate differently.

## Daily life and load grammar

A carried object changes the whole body, not only the hand. Author separate motion
families for water, grain, timber, cloth, child-carry, weapon, saddle/tack and baggage.
Women and men should not be built from one neutral walk with a prop parented to a bone.

## Water edge

Water references are valuable for **occupation density**: bathing, vessels, seated work,
conversation, waiting, shade and circulation coexist at the edge. The water itself does
not imply that every depicted ritual, garment or social relation belongs in our period.

## Court and procession

Court paintings are strongest for blocking:

- principal seated or otherwise visually central;
- attendants reinforcing silhouette rather than random idle clusters;
- scribes/documents occupying a different layer;
- weapons as social objects even when unequipped;
- animals/procession elements on the perimeter;
- negative space expressing access.

Ranjit's art direction remains: the environment and entourage can gain spectacle faster
than his own personal ornament.

## Weapons and equipment

Do not reduce an armed person to equipment slots. Build **load-bearing asymmetry**:
sash, scabbard, pouch, pistol/tool, cloth knot, shield, bow or staff can make one side
visually heavier. Object references with unverified provenance remain C-tier until a
museum/catalogue source is attached.

## Material memory

Industrial generation may create wear variants. Direct art direction decides why one
wear pattern matters:

- hand-polished chair arm;
- scabbard throat repaired in unlike metal;
- tack patched twice;
- cloth protected under a belt retaining stronger dye;
- rotated carpet;
- remounted jewel;
- cup kept for someone absent.

These details should survive LOD and content-generation passes because they encode story,
not noise.

## Religious / monumental sites

Use the corpus to compose exterior approach: threshold, sound, water reflection, crowd
slowing, vendors/attendants, shade and reveal. Existing project rule remains:
religious sites are non-enterable/view-only unless a separately approved design changes
that rule. The experience can still be extraordinary.

## Production contract for future industrialization

A generator may propose thousands of streets, courts, garments or props. It must receive:

1. historical/reference tier,
2. allowed influences,
3. forbidden inferences,
4. geometric grammar,
5. material-memory constraints,
6. authoritative gameplay clearance.

The generator supplies **possibility space**. Human/direct craft retains authority over
meaningful exceptions, final composition, performance, beauty and historical judgment.
